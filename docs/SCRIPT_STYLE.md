# Short Script Style — LOCKED (reverse-engineered from the reference viral short)

The narration is one breathless chain written to be spoken in a rush.

1. **Breathless chain, past tense.** Beats linked by So / But / That's when /
   Luckily / And then — almost no full stops. Every connector is a micro
   cliffhanger: "but" promises a reversal, "that's when" promises a payoff.
2. **Hook in the first 2 seconds.** Line 1 is the most absurd version of the
   premise, stated flat. No setup, no greeting, no "in a world where".
   The hook caption repeats line 1.
3. **One idea per beat, and every beat is visual.** ~4–9 spoken words per
   beat; if a beat can't be shown in one panel, rewrite it until it can.
   The script is pre-cut at writing time (this is what makes 1–2s cuts work).
4. **Escalation ladder, not plot summary.** Beats climb: each one slightly
   more extreme than the last. Anything that doesn't raise the stakes
   (backstory, explanations) is cut or compressed into half a clause.
   Chronological completeness is NOT required — the strongest contrast
   chain wins, even if it pulls a beat from a later chapter (flag it in
   the EDL when it does).
5. **Contrast pairs are the engine.** Humiliation vs power, before vs after:
   "saved the world / they took everything", "robbed him / begged him",
   "any man would have begged / he refused".
6. **Names are rationed.** Characters enter by role ("a young agent",
   "the officials", "the gods"), never by name — Shorts viewers don't know
   the series. The only name spoken is the series title, saved for the
   final line: "The name is <Series>."
7. **Cliffhanger ending, never resolution.** Stop at the most charged
   moment. The on-screen end question continues the story in the comments.
8. **`[music]` markers are written into the script** at emotional turns
   (impact sting, tone shift, outro swell). They are edit cues, never spoken.

Shape: ~25 beats / ~200 words ≈ 55s at 1.3x. Target 45–100s.
